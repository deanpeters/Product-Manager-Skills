# EOL Internal Enablement Example — Industrial

**Product:** NFA-200 series retrofit controller (Northfield Automation)
**Level:** 3 — Heavy
**Context:** ~120 installations, 8 channel partners, service contracts through 2028, UL 508A and CE
certified. NFA-500 is the successor but needs a different mounting bracket — every migration is a
scoped site visit. This is an **End-of-Sale** announcement with no EOL date.

**What makes this Level 3:** field service techs and channel partners are customer-facing here, not
just Sales and Support. The pack has to arm four audiences, and the wrong word about certification
creates a compliance problem rather than an unhappy call.

---

```markdown
## EOL Internal Enablement Pack: NFA-200 Series Retrofit Controller

**Level**: 3 -- Heavy
**Materials included**: Support FAQ, sales talking points, objection
handling, escalation playbook, channel partner brief, training outline
**Announcement target date**: March 31, 2026
**Enablement must be complete by**: March 13, 2026 (partners briefed
by March 6)

---

### 1. Support and Field Service FAQ

#### What is happening?
- Q: Is the NFA-200 being discontinued?
- A: It's going end-of-sale March 31, 2027. Installed units keep
  running and stay under service through your agreement.

- Q: When does my controller stop working?
- A: It doesn't. We are not setting an end-of-life date and we will
  never remotely disable an installed unit.

#### What does this mean for me?
- Q: Does this affect my running line?
- A: No. Service, parts, and support continue under your existing
  agreement.

- Q: Can I still buy more NFA-200 units?
- A: Through March 31, 2027. After that, the NFA-500 is the product.

#### What are my options?
- Q: Do I have to switch to the NFA-500?
- A: Not until your service agreement ends. When you're ready, we'll
  scope the retrofit with you at no charge.

- Q: Is the NFA-500 a drop-in replacement?
- A: No. It uses a different mounting standard and panel depth. Every
  migration is a scoped site visit -- budget it as a project.

#### What about my data?
- Q: What happens to controller telemetry and logs?
- A: Retained through end of service, December 31, 2028. We'll export
  your records on request at any time.

#### What about my contract?
- Q: Is my service agreement still valid?
- A: Yes, to its full stated term, including agreements running into
  2028. Contact your account manager for written confirmation.

- Q: Can I renew my service agreement?
- A: Renewals stop December 31, 2027. Existing terms are honored past
  that date.

#### What about certification?
- Q: Does this affect our UL or CE compliance?
- A: No. Installed NFA-200 units retain UL 508A and CE marking. This
  status change does not affect your compliance posture.

- Q: We run a validated process -- what do we need?
- A: We'll supply a change-control documentation package for your
  quality records. Request it from your account manager.

#### When does support end?
- Q: When do firmware updates stop?
- A: Non-safety updates stop December 31, 2028. Safety-related updates
  continue to end of service.

- Q: When does service end?
- A: December 31, 2028, or your agreement end date if later.

#### Escalation path
- Level 1: Field service desk -- standard questions, parts orders
- Level 2: Ravi (Service Manager) -- site-specific concerns, scheduling
- Level 3: Elena (VP Service) -- contract disputes, escalated accounts
- Executive: Tom (COO) -- top 10 accounts, regulatory inquiries, press

### 2. Sales Talking Points

#### Positioning the Transition
- Frame: Consolidating manufacturing onto one platform
- Avoid: "Obsolete," "unsupported," "end of life"
- Lead with: Your installed units are not affected

#### Product Comparison

| Capability | NFA-200 | NFA-500 |
|---|---|---|
| Safety interlock behavior | Yes | Yes -- equivalent |
| Ladder-logic compatibility | Yes | Yes |
| Field-serviceable design | Yes | Yes |
| Modular I/O expansion | Fixed | Yes |
| Remote diagnostics | No | Yes |
| Firmware updates without site visit | No | Yes |
| Mounting compatibility | -- | No -- different bracket and panel
  depth; site visit required per unit |
| Spare parts availability | Through Dec 2028 | Current |

#### Pipeline Impact Guidance
- Deals in progress: honor open NFA-200 quotes to March 31, 2027
- Planned expansions: raise last-time-buy now, not in 2027
- Multi-site standardization: move to NFA-500 or plan mixed fleet
- New prospects asking: quote NFA-500 only

#### Competitive Response
- If a competitor raises our EOS: we committed to service through 2028
  and set no shutdown date
- If asked about the mounting change: confirm it directly, then offer
  the free retrofit assessment

### 3. Objection Handling

#### "We standardized on the NFA-200 across six sites."
- Acknowledge: That's a real investment and this complicates it
- Reframe: Nothing installed is affected, and last-time-buy runs to
  March 2027 -- you can complete planned sites on the 200
- Offer: Last-time-buy quantities reserved at current pricing, plus a
  free multi-site retrofit roadmap (approved: reservations to March 2027)

#### "The NFA-500 doesn't fit our panels."
- Acknowledge: Correct. Different bracket and panel depth. I won't
  soften that
- Reframe: It's a scoped site visit, not a swap -- which is exactly why
  we're not setting an EOL date. You retrofit on your schedule
- Offer: Free site assessment with per-site cost and downtime window,
  whether or not you commit (approved for all installations)

#### "We just signed a three-year service agreement."
- Acknowledge: And we'll honor every month of it
- Reframe: The agreement covers service, parts, and safety updates to
  its full term. End-of-sale doesn't touch it
- Offer: Written confirmation of your end date from Legal (approved --
  standard letter available)

#### "Are we going to fail an audit over this?"
- Acknowledge: A fair concern with certified equipment
- Reframe: UL 508A and CE marking remain valid on installed units.
  End-of-sale is a commercial status, not a certification change
- Offer: Change-control documentation package for your quality records
  (approved -- Regulatory supplies within 10 business days)

#### "Will you brick our controllers like other vendors have?"
- Acknowledge: That's happened in this industry and the concern is earned
- Reframe: We have set no end-of-life date and will never remotely
  disable an installed unit. That's in the customer notice in writing
- Offer: Point them to the written commitment in the announcement
  (approved -- quote it directly)

#### "Can you extend spare parts past 2028?"
- Acknowledge: I understand you're planning past that
- Reframe: Parts are committed through December 2028. Beyond that we
  can't commit, because component supply is outside our control
- Offer: Quarterly parts-stock levels published so you can plan, plus a
  final buy option in 2028 (do not promise parts past 2028)

### 4. Channel Partner Brief

#### What partners need to know
- Last-time-buy window closes March 31, 2027
- Inventory buyback available through June 2027
- Service and warranty obligations unchanged
- Partners briefed March 6, public notice March 31

#### What partners can tell their customers
- Installed units are unaffected and stay serviced
- No end-of-life date has been set
- NFA-500 requires a site-specific retrofit
- Free retrofit assessments available now

#### What partners should NOT do
- Do not quote an end-of-life or shutdown date
- Do not promise NFA-500 drop-in compatibility
- Do not commit parts availability past December 2028
- Do not discount to clear inventory before buyback terms
```

## What to notice

- **The FAQ gains a section the SaaS version doesn't have.** "What about certification?" sits
  between contract and support questions because in a regulated context it drives real call volume
  — and a wrong answer creates a compliance problem, not just an unhappy customer.
- **Two objections end with an explicit prohibition.** "Do not promise parts past 2028" and the
  partner brief's forbidden-claims list. At Level 3 the damage from an invented commitment is
  contractual, so the pack forbids as clearly as it authorizes.
- **The bricking objection is answered by pointing at the written notice.** The strongest response
  isn't a talking point, it's a quotable sentence the company already published. Enablement works
  best when it points at commitments rather than generating new ones.
- **Partners are briefed 25 days before the public notice.** March 6 versus March 31, stated in the
  header. Resellers with their own customer relationships need the lead time to prepare their own
  conversations.
- **Training allocates 20 minutes to role-play.** The longest block. Reading objection handlers
  silently doesn't build the reflex — and "will you brick our controllers" is a question you want a
  rep to have already answered out loud once.
