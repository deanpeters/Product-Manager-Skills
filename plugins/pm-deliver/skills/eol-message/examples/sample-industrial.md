# EOL Message Example — Industrial

**Product:** NFA-200 series retrofit controller (Northfield Automation)
**Size:** Full · **Path:** Replacement, with channel and regulatory obligations
**Context:** ~120 installations, 8 channel partners reselling, service contracts running through
2028, UL/CE certified, spare parts inventory in the field. The NFA-500 platform is the successor
but is **not** a drop-in — it needs a different mounting bracket and a per-site retrofit.

**Why this is an End-of-Sale announcement, not an End-of-Life one.** A readiness assessment on the
NFA-200 landed on *hold the EOL, take the EOS now*: the pressure to retire was internal
(manufacturing wanted the line back), the service obligations run to 2028, and there is no
drop-in migration path yet. So this message stops selling the product and commits to servicing it —
it does not tell 120 sites their controllers are going dark. **Announcing a full EOL here would be
announcing a date you cannot keep.**

---

## The message

```markdown
## Product Status Change: NFA-200 Series Retrofit Controller

### Product Transition Narrative

**We are:** Northfield Automation, supplier of retrofit control systems for legacy production lines
- We commit to supporting equipment for the life of the service agreement attached to it
- We modernize our platforms without stranding the installed base
- We're building toward controllers that extend line life rather than forcing line replacement

**Announcing:**
- Effective March 31, 2027, the NFA-200 series will no longer be available for purchase. **Your
  installed NFA-200 units are not affected.** We will continue to service, support, and supply
  parts for every NFA-200 under contract through the end of that agreement.

**Because:**
- The NFA-500 platform supersedes the NFA-200 for new installations, with modular I/O and
  IIoT connectivity the 200 series cannot be retrofitted to provide
- Concentrating manufacturing on one platform lets us hold parts availability and lead times for
  the installed base rather than splitting capacity across two lines
- Consolidating certification and firmware maintenance onto one platform keeps our compliance
  work focused where it protects you

**Which means for you:**
- Nothing changes for your running lines — service, parts, and firmware support continue under
  your existing agreement
- Parts availability for the NFA-200 improves, not degrades, as production capacity consolidates
- When you're ready to modernize, the NFA-500 is available and we'll scope the retrofit with you

### Current Product Context

**Our product** the NFA-200 series retrofit controller
- **is a** drop-in control module for legacy production equipment
- **that has served** manufacturers extending the life of existing lines since 2016
- **by providing** modern control and safety interlocks without replacing working machinery

### Customer Impact

**We understand that this may affect you by:**
- Requiring you to plan NFA-500 evaluation before your service agreement expires, rather than
  after
- Limiting your ability to standardize new lines on the NFA-200 after March 31, 2027 — if you have
  planned expansions on the 200, talk to us now
- Requiring a site-specific retrofit when you do move: the NFA-500 uses a different mounting
  standard and is not a like-for-like swap. We will not pretend otherwise.

### Transition Solution

**For** manufacturers running retrofit control on legacy lines
- **that currently use** the NFA-200 series
- the NFA-500 platform
- **is a** modular retrofit controller with IIoT connectivity
- **that** performs the same control and interlock functions you rely on today, with remote
  diagnostics and modular I/O you can expand without replacing the unit

### Differentiation and Continuity

- **Like** the NFA-200,
- the NFA-500
- **provides** the same safety interlock behavior, the same ladder-logic compatibility, and the
  same field-serviceable design
- **while also offering** modular I/O expansion, remote diagnostics, and firmware updates that
  don't require a site visit

**What is not continuous, stated plainly:** the NFA-500 requires a different mounting bracket and
panel depth. Every migration is a scoped site visit. Budget for it as a project, not a swap.

### Support and Next Steps

**To ensure a smooth transition, we will:**
- Honor every existing NFA-200 service agreement to its full term, including agreements running
  into 2028
- Hold NFA-200 spare parts inventory through December 31, 2028, and publish quarterly parts-stock
  levels so you can plan
- Provide a free site retrofit assessment for any NFA-200 installation, whether or not you commit
  to the NFA-500
- Apply a trade-in credit against NFA-500 hardware for NFA-200 units retired before their
  agreement expires
- Continue issuing safety-related firmware updates for the NFA-200 through end of service

### Timeline

| Gate | Date | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Notice of Status Change | March 31, 2026 | This announcement. Nothing changes operationally. |
| End of Sale | March 31, 2027 | New NFA-200 units can no longer be ordered. Installed units unaffected. |
| End of Expansion | March 31, 2027 | No new I/O expansion modules for the NFA-200. Existing configurations continue. |
| End of Maintenance | December 31, 2028 | Non-safety firmware updates stop. Safety updates continue to end of service. |
| End of Service | December 31, 2028 | Service agreements, parts supply, and support obligations conclude. |

**End of Life is not scheduled.** Your NFA-200 will keep running as long as it runs. We are not
setting a date on which your equipment stops working, and we will not remotely disable installed
units — ever.

### Contractual and Regulatory Notes

- **Service agreements:** All NFA-200 agreements are honored to their stated term. Agreements
  extending past December 31, 2028 will be honored to term; contact your account manager to
  confirm your end date in writing.
- **Certification:** NFA-200 units retain UL 508A and CE marking. This status change does not
  affect the certification of installed equipment or your compliance posture.
- **Validated environments:** For customers operating under validated processes, we will supply
  change-control documentation for your quality records on request.
- **Last-time buy:** Orders for NFA-200 units and expansion modules will be accepted through
  March 31, 2027. Contact your channel partner or account manager to place a final order.

### Call to Action

- **Running NFA-200 units?** No action required. Confirm your service end date at
  northfield-automation.com/agreements
- **Planning expansion on the NFA-200?** Contact your account manager before March 31, 2027 to
  discuss last-time-buy quantities
- **Ready to evaluate the NFA-500?** Request a free retrofit assessment at
  northfield-automation.com/nfa500-assessment
- Questions: service@northfield-automation.com or your channel partner
```

## Why this works

- **It announces what is true.** EOS with an open-ended EOL, because the retrofit path doesn't
  exist yet. A confident EOL date here would have been a promise to break in 2028.
- **The headline defuses the panic.** "Your installed NFA-200 units are not affected" sits in the
  announcement itself, not four sections down. In industrial contexts the first question is always
  *does my line stop*, and the message answers it before it's asked.
- **The discontinuity is named without hedging.** "Every migration is a scoped site visit. Budget
  for it as a project, not a swap." Sales will hate this line. It prevents a year of disputes.
- **Obligations are addressed, not just acknowledged.** Contract terms, certification status,
  change-control documentation, and last-time-buy each get a specific commitment — this is the
  section a Brief or Standard message doesn't have, and the reason this one is Full.
- **"We will not remotely disable installed units — ever."** The single most damaging EOL failures
  on record involve vendors bricking hardware customers had paid for. Saying the opposite, in
  writing, is worth the sentence.
- **Channel partners have a path.** Last-time-buy routes through them explicitly, so the eight
  resellers aren't finding out from their own customers.

---

## What this example deliberately does not do

- **It doesn't set an EOL date to look decisive.** The gate table stops at End of Service and says
  why. Naming a date you can't keep is worse than admitting you don't have one.
- **It doesn't oversell the NFA-500.** The continuity section lists what genuinely carries over,
  then breaks out the mounting incompatibility separately rather than burying it in a bullet.
- **It doesn't lead with manufacturing capacity.** That was the real internal trigger. It appears
  only as a customer-facing benefit that happens to be true — parts availability for the installed
  base improves. If it hadn't been true, it wouldn't be in the message.
