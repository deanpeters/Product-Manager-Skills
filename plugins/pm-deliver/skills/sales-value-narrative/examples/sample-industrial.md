# Sales Value Narrative Example: Industrial

**Offering:** NFA-500 retrofit controller (Northfield Automation)
**Sold to:** plant engineering and maintenance; buyers are procurement and the plant manager; 8 partners
**Alternatives that win:** "our current supplier is fine," and keeping the NFA-200 running

**Why this example exists:** the status quo here is a *working machine*, so the why-now is a
supportability cliff, and the narrative must reach procurement and finance, not just engineers.

---

```markdown
## Narrative: NFA-500

**Why change**
The NFA-200 works. The cost is what happens when it stops: replacement parts in six to eight
weeks as stock thins, and the people who can program it are retiring. Cost of staying put =
hours of downtime per failure x cost per hour x expected failures per year (customer's numbers).
`[NEEDS SOURCE]` for failure rates; use the customer's maintenance logs.

**Why now**
Triggers: spare-parts coverage and service contracts for the NFA-200 base run to 2028; a
planned shutdown window this year; a retirement of the one engineer who knows the program; a
customer audit asking for a support plan.

**Why us**
Upgrade the line without stopping it: a shift, not a shutdown; connected fault warning; your
programs come with you (import tested, report T-114). Other pillar proof `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

## Value by role
| Role | Cares about | Value | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maintenance engineer | Programs and wiring they trust | Imports existing programs | T-114 |
| Plant manager | Output, no lost weekends | Swap in a shift | Pilot install times `[NEEDS SOURCE]` |
| Procurement | Supplier risk, terms | Parts and service in six countries; service through 2028 | Service agreement terms |
| Finance | Payback | Avoided downtime vs project cost | Repeatable calculation |
| Distributor | Margin, stocking | Bracket kit keeps installs predictable | Partner terms |

## Objections
| Objection | Behind it | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| "Our current supplier is fine" | No trigger to move | "Is parts coverage on your current unit confirmed past 2028? Let's check together." |
| "Price" | Over budget, or a competitor's lower quote | Clarify; compare total cost of ownership, including downtime avoided |
| "Will it integrate with what we have?" | Technical evaluator's fear | Walk through program import and the bracket kit; offer a site survey |
| "Is the connected tier a cybersecurity risk?" | IT/OT security | Share architecture note (`[NEEDS SOURCE]`: security review in progress) |

## Do-not-say (dated Apr 15)
- "Drop-in replacement" (it needs a different bracket)
- "Most reliable retrofit controller" (no comparative data)

## Cards needed
Status quo (keep the NFA-200 running) first; then the competitor named in 3 of 8 lost deals.
```

## What to learn from this

- **"Drop-in replacement" is on the do-not-say list** because the bracket differs. One careless
  phrase would turn an installation visit into a complaint.
- **The security objection has no answer yet,** and the narrative says so instead of improvising.
- **Procurement and finance each get their own value block.** They decide, and they never see the demo.
