Everything in this repository (every skill, template, example, and doc) is licensed under [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](LICENSE). This page explains, in plain language, how that applies to the most common situations. It is written so you can cite it to a manager or a legal team.

## Quick answers

| Your situation | Permitted? |
|---|---|
| Use the skills in your day job at a for-profit company (PRDs, discovery, strategy, and so on) | ✅ Yes |
| Load them into your own AI agent setup (Claude, Codex, OpenCode, etc.) | ✅ Yes |

## Internal use at a for-profit company

**Using these skills as tools in your daily work at a for-profit employer is within the intent of this license.** "NonCommercial" here means *do not sell the skills or build a paid offering out of them*. It does not mean your employer must be a nonprofit. A manufacturer, a health-tech company, a bank, and a SaaS company can all have their PMs use these skills to do their jobs.

The line is what you do with the **skills themselves**:

- Using them to do your work: fine.
- Selling them, or wrapping them into something you sell (a paid course, a consulting deliverable that *is* the skills, a commercial product or service): needs written permission.

## Who owns what you produce with them

**What you produce by *using* the skills is yours.** The PRD, analysis, roadmap, or interview guide you create with a skill is your work product (or your employer's, per your employment terms). It is not an adaptation of the skill, and the share-alike requirement does not attach to it.

Share-alike applies when you distribute **an adapted version of the skills themselves**: a modified `SKILL.md`, a forked template, a remixed skill pack. In that case, license your adaptation under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and credit the original.

## Attribution

If you share the skills or an adaptation outside your organization, credit **Dean Peters, [Product-Manager-Skills](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills)**, link the license, and note what you changed.

## Not sure?

[Open an issue](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/issues) and describe your use. If you're using these skills to get better at the craft and to help others do the same, the answer is almost certainly yes. For commercial uses (selling, bundling, paid training), ask first and get written permission.
